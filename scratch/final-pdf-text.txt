Abstract

Employee onboarding requires coordination among company administrators, managers, and new employees.
Policies, tasks, training, meetings, assessments, and questions are often spread across separate tools, making it
difficult to maintain a consistent process and see where support is needed. Ascent is a multi-tenant web
application that organizes these activities in company workspaces with role-based access.
The platform supports company membership, departments, training batches, task assignment and review,
meeting schedules, worksheets, submissions, employee discussions, audit records, and performance analytics.
Its AI layer uses the Gemini API for three advisory workflows: retrieval-grounded answers from company
onboarding material, provisional grading suggestions for written assessment answers, and summaries of
recurring forum questions. For the onboarding assistant, managers paste text into a company knowledge page;
the server chunks the content, creates embeddings, stores them with the company identifier, retrieves relevant
passages for an employee query, and sends those passages to a language model. Source titles are returned with
the answer.
The implementation uses Next.js, React, TypeScript, Clerk, Prisma, and Neon PostgreSQL. The latest
automated test run passed 69 of 69 tests. Three fictional company documents were successfully indexed as
three searchable chunks in the Ascent Demo Workspace. The current version does not provide PDF upload and
extraction, anonymous forum posting, or a separate scenario-simulation engine. These gaps and the limits of
the verification are documented in this report.

Keywords: employee onboarding, multi-tenant systems, role-based access control, retrieval-augmented generation, Gemini,
performance analytics

Chapter 1: Introduction

1.1 Background

Employee onboarding brings together account setup, company policies, role expectations, training,
meetings, assignments, and feedback. In many organizations these activities are spread across email,
shared folders, calendars, and informal conversations. New employees can have difficulty knowing
what to do first, where to find authoritative guidance, and whom to ask when a question feels basic.
Managers may also have limited visibility into work completion and recurring points of confusion.

Ascent is a web-based employee onboarding and performance platform. It gives each company a
separate workspace and organizes people, departments, cohorts, tasks, meetings, assessments,
discussions, and progress information within that workspace. The current implementation also
includes AI-assisted features: a company-material question-answering assistant, provisional grading
suggestions for written answers, and summaries of recurring forum questions.

The report uses the supplied project statement as the requirements baseline and the repository
implementation as the delivery baseline. The project statement calls for role-based onboarding,
practical work, anonymous questions, manager insights, and a company-specific assistant. The
implementation meets many of these goals, with the differences recorded in Chapter 5.

1.2 Problem Statement

When onboarding depends on disconnected documents and manual follow-up, new employees may
receive inconsistent guidance and managers may not see incomplete work or repeated questions in
time. A single role-aware system can organize company content, assign tasks and learning activities,
schedule meetings, capture questions, and provide a consistent way to review progress. The system
must keep each organization’s records isolated and give each user only the actions allowed by their
role.

1.3 Motivation

The project combines full-stack development with a practical organizational problem. It applies
tenant-aware data modeling, role-based authorization, task and meeting workflows, assessments,
analytics, and AI retrieval to a unified onboarding experience. The AI features are designed to
support people: the assistant answers from company material, while managers retain control over
grading and follow-up decisions.

1.5 Scope of the Project

The application is a software prototype for employee onboarding and performance management. It
supports Platform Admin, Company Admin, Manager, and Employee roles. It includes tenant-scoped
data access, invitation and membership workflows, task assignment and review, meetings and
attendance, worksheets and evaluations, discussion threads, audit records, analytics, and three AI-
assisted workflows.

The current knowledge-base interface accepts pasted text. It does not provide a file upload or PDF
extraction pipeline. Work can be assigned through tasks, training activities, assessments, and
historical completion records, but the application does not include a separate interactive scenario
simulator. Forum threads identify their authors; anonymous posting from the project statement is not
yet implemented. The application is not connected to production HR information systems or real
company identity directories.

1.6 Significance of the Project

Ascent demonstrates how onboarding activities can be managed in a single tenant-aware application.
It provides a traceable workflow from assignment to submission and review, gives managers
operational indicators, and lets employees ask questions in context. The assistant can reduce the time
needed to locate policy information when the answer exists in the company’s indexed material. These
are prototype capabilities; their effectiveness in a live organization depends on accurate source
content, access configuration, and continued human review.

1.7 Stakeholders and Operational Context
The platform serves four levels of responsibility. The Platform Admin is responsible for the platform
as a whole, while the Company Admin manages a single organization's workspace and membership.
A Manager works with assigned employees, reviews work, schedules support, and interprets
progress. An Employee completes assigned onboarding activities, asks questions, and receives
feedback. These responsibilities inform the separation of global role and company role in the data
model.
The distinction between a company administrator and a manager is useful operationally. Company
administration includes maintaining the organization, adding members, configuring departments or
cohorts, and managing shared company guidance. A manager focuses on a team: assigning activities,
reviewing submissions, organizing meetings, and identifying support needs. An employee interacts
with the resources and assigned work that are relevant to their own onboarding. The application still
relies on accurate membership and reporting relationships; incorrect setup can result in incomplete
views or access denials.
1.8 Project Outputs and Evaluation Boundary
The implemented outputs are a browser-based application, a relational database schema, server-side
workflow actions, AI service integrations, automated logic tests, and seeded demonstration data. The
main deliverable is not a trained foundation model. Gemini is called as an external service for text
embeddings and generated outputs. The application's contribution is the orchestration around those
calls: choosing the company data, selecting relevant context, validating responses, and presenting the
result through an onboarding interface.
The evidence in this report is divided into implementation evidence and outcome evidence.
Implementation evidence includes source modules, database structures, authorization checks, tests,
and the successful indexing of three synthetic demo documents. Outcome evidence would require
user studies, verified production content, measured completion time, answer-quality evaluation, and
comparison with an existing onboarding process. The latter has not been collected, so no claims
about improved retention or employee productivity are made.

Chapter 2: Literature Survey

2.1 Introduction to Employee Onboarding Systems

Digital onboarding systems typically combine employee records, policy access, training, checklists,
scheduling, and progress tracking. A document repository alone can distribute information, but it
does not show whether the employee completed required activities. A learning-management system
can track courses, but it may not manage company-specific assignments, meetings, employee
questions, and manager reviews in one workflow. Ascent combines these functions in a multi-role
application.

2.2 Role-Based Access and Multi-Tenant Applications

In a multi-tenant service, each organization is a data boundary. A user may belong to one or more
companies, and their permissions depend on the active company membership. Ascent stores a
company identifier on the major operational records and resolves an active membership before
serving company pages or performing mutations. Role checks are made in server-side authorization
helpers and actions; hiding a navigation item alone is not treated as authorization.

The application distinguishes a global Platform Admin role from company-level Company Admin,
Manager, and Employee roles. Managers receive scoped actions for team work and review, while
employees receive personal tasks and assessment workflows. These are application roles; they are
not a configurable simulation of every job family listed in the project statement.

2.3 Practical Work, Assessment, and Feedback

Task and assessment systems make onboarding more active by asking a new employee to complete
work and receive feedback. Ascent supports operational and training tasks, historical training records,
mixed worksheets, submissions, objective auto-grading, manager rubrics, resubmission, and grade
release. This provides practical work and assessment workflows. A future scenario engine could add
branching cases, simulated artifacts, scoring rules, and safe sandboxes for role-specific situations.

2.4 Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Company Guidance

Retrieval-Augmented Generation combines a retrieval step with language generation. A query is used
to find relevant external passages, and those passages are supplied to a language model as context.
Lewis et al. describe this approach for knowledge-intensive NLP tasks. Ascent uses a lightweight
company-scoped form of this pattern: text is divided into chunks, each chunk is embedded, relevant
stored chunks are ranked by cosine similarity, and the most relevant passages are supplied to Gemini
with instructions to answer from that material.

                                                                      Ascent | Page 13
This approach can make answers specific to a company’s policies without fine-tuning a model on
every policy change. Its limits are also clear: retrieval quality depends on document quality and
chunking, and the generated response can still be incomplete. Ascent therefore shows source titles
and instructs the assistant to refer employees to a manager when the material does not contain an
answer.

2.5 Related AI Assistance for Managers

Generative models can help summarize repeated questions and draft feedback, but consequential
decisions should remain with the responsible manager. Ascent’s assessment suggestion feature
returns provisional marks and feedback for written responses. The manager can review and edit the
suggestions before saving an evaluation. Forum insight analysis groups open or answered threads
into themes and proposes follow-up actions. These results are advisory and should be checked
against the source records.

2.6 Research Gap and Project Position

The project statement identifies a gap between static onboarding information and practical, role-
specific learning with visible manager support. Ascent addresses the coordination, task assignment,
assessment, meeting, discussion, and progress portions of that gap. It also provides company-
grounded AI answers and manager summaries. The gap is only partially closed: file-based policy
ingestion, anonymous questions, automated role-specific roadmap generation, and a dedicated work-
simulation engine remain future work.

2.8 Design Principles Derived from the Survey

The literature and the project statement suggest several design principles. First, onboarding should be
represented as an ongoing process with tasks, resources, contact, and feedback rather than as a single
document handoff. Second, employees and managers need different views because their
responsibilities are different. Third, company identity must constrain data retrieval and mutation.
Fourth, AI-generated guidance should be grounded in maintained company material and present
source context. Fifth, decisions that affect assessment or employee standing should retain human
review. Finally, evaluation must distinguish software correctness from organizational effectiveness.

These principles guide the implementation review. The report examines not only whether a page
exists, but also whether a data model records the relevant state, whether an action enforces the proper
role, whether a manager can review an AI suggestion, and whether evidence exists for the stated
behavior.

3.1 Proposed Methodology

The project follows an iterative application-development process:

  1. Requirement analysis: identify the needs of company administrators, managers, and employees
     from the project statement.

  2. Domain modeling: represent users, memberships, companies, departments, tasks, meetings,
     assessments, discussions, and analytics as related database entities.

  3. Authorization design: resolve active membership and company context on the server, then
     apply global and company role checks.

  4. Workflow implementation: build server-rendered pages and server actions for company
     administration, onboarding work, meetings, assessments, discussions, and reporting.

  5. AI integration: connect server-side actions to Gemini for text generation and embeddings; keep
     retrieved knowledge scoped to the current company.

  6. Verification: run automated unit and logic tests for validation, scoring, access boundaries,
     schedule conflicts, assessment safeguards, and AI text utilities.

3.4 Feasibility Analysis

Technical feasibility: The project uses a conventional web stack and managed PostgreSQL database.
Gemini calls are made from server-side code. The main technical constraints are external service
availability, careful tenant filtering, and the current absence of a file parser or vector database
extension.

Operational feasibility: Administrators and managers can use familiar browser workflows to invite
users, organize teams, assign work, schedule meetings, and enter policy text. Employees use a
dashboard rather than switching between multiple onboarding tools. A company must still supply and
maintain accurate onboarding material.

Economic feasibility: The prototype uses managed services and an API-based model. Operating cost
depends on provider plans, database usage, model traffic, email volume, and hosting. A costed
production deployment has not been established in the repository.

3.5 System Architecture

Ascent is a Next.js application. The browser submits page requests and server actions to the Next.js
server. Clerk supplies the normal user identity. The server resolves a local user, active membership,
company, and role, then reads or writes company records through Prisma and Neon PostgreSQL. AI
actions call Gemini from the server. The client never receives the Gemini secret key or performs
database queries directly.

The administrator creates or prepares a company workspace, defines departments and cohorts, and
invites or provisions members. Managers configure assignments, assessments, meetings, and content.
Employees complete assigned work, submit assessments, attend meetings, and ask questions.

3.6.1 Company Knowledge and Assistant Workflow

An administrator or manager enters a document title and pastes policy or onboarding text into the
Knowledge Base. The server validates the length, splits the text into chunks of approximately 1,200
characters, obtains a Gemini embedding for each chunk, and stores the text and embedding with the
company and creator membership. When an employee asks a question, the server embeds the query,
computes cosine similarity against that company’s stored chunks, selects up to four passages, and
asks Gemini to answer using only those passages. The UI displays the answer and source titles.

3.7 System Constraints

The project requires a configured database and Clerk environment for normal authentication. AI
functions require a valid Gemini API key and external API access. Test identities are local seeded
users and are intended for demonstration only. Knowledge documents are plaintext entries rather than
uploaded files. The standard automated suite tests application logic but does not replace browser
acceptance testing, security review, or production monitoring.

3.8 Requirements Elicitation and Analysis

The supplied statement is the primary requirements source. It identifies company administration,
role-specific onboarding plans, historical work simulations, scheduling, trainee dashboards,
anonymous doubts, AI categorization, a company-specific assistant, and progress insights.
Requirements were grouped by actor and workflow, then compared with application routes, server
actions, schema models, and automated tests. This method creates a trace from the stated need to the
delivered code and avoids treating planned work as completed functionality.

The employee journey was analyzed from access to completion. An employee must first authenticate
and resolve a company membership; then see assigned work, attend sessions, complete assessments,
ask for information, submit questions, and receive feedback. The manager journey begins with a
company-scoped view of members and includes assignment, scheduling, review, and progress
interpretation. The company administrator journey includes maintaining the workspace, members,
teams, and shared policy text. The Platform Admin journey is distinct because it is not attached to a
single employee team.

The analysis also identifies cross-cutting constraints. Every company operation needs tenant context.
Any mutation needs input validation. Sensitive administrative changes should be auditable. AI access
requires secret configuration and external network availability. A report or dashboard value needs a
defined calculation and should not be interpreted without considering data completeness. These
constraints appear in the non-functional requirements and verification plan.

3.10.1 Company administrator workflow

The administrator signs in, enters an active company workspace, checks membership and
organization structure, and assigns the appropriate managers to employee profiles. The administrator
can maintain the company knowledge text and oversee the workspace. For a demonstration, the
seeded Ascent Demo Workspace has a dedicated manager-facing identity and employee-facing

3.10.2 Manager workflow
The manager enters the company dashboard, reviews the team, creates tasks or worksheets, selects
employees or cohorts, and sets timing and instructions. The manager creates meetings and considers

delivery timing, and released assessment outcomes according to documented weights. Managers
should open the source records and consider assignment difficulty, due-date changes, and missing

validated measure of employee potential.

3.10.3 Employee workflow
The employee signs in, enters the company workspace, and reviews their dashboard and tasks. For
each assignment, the employee reads the instructions, updates progress, and submits evidence. An
assessment may be saved as a draft before submission. A manager reviews the answer and releases
evaluation when ready. The employee can attend scheduled sessions, participate in named
discussions, and ask the company assistant questions against indexed material.

When a source does not answer a question, the desired assistant behavior is to state that the material
does not specify an answer and refer the employee to a manager. The employee can inspect returned
source titles. Because forum posts are named, employees should use the discussion area only for
questions appropriate to a named company forum; the current implementation does not satisfy the
statement's anonymity requirement.

3.12 Feasibility and Risk Analysis

Technical feasibility is supported by the existing implementation and the use of widely adopted web
and database components. The principal technical risks are cross-company access errors, external AI
provider failure, provider response variability, large knowledge corpora that make application-side
vector comparison expensive, and incomplete coverage of proposed simulation features. These risks
can be tested and mitigated, but the current prototype does not establish production-grade capacity or
availability.

Operational feasibility depends on a company assigning an owner to onboarding content, maintaining
role memberships, and responding to manager review queues. The system cannot keep a policy
accurate if the company does not update it. It cannot provide meaningful analytics if tasks are not
consistently assigned or assessed. The deployment must also account for email delivery, identity
configuration, database credentials, and the availability of the AI provider.

Economic feasibility is suitable for an academic prototype because the development stack is based on
open-source libraries and managed service tiers. Production cost cannot be inferred from the code
alone. It will depend on active users, document volume, embedding operations, question volume,
model token usage, database storage, email delivery, hosting, and support. A cost and latency study
should precede a production rollout.

4.9 Company Knowledge Base and AI Assistant

4.14 Request Lifecycle and Module Boundaries

mutation is submitted to a server action. The action first calls an authorization helper, then validates
the input, applies business rules, performs a database operation, and revalidates the route or returns
structured feedback. The browser does not connect directly to PostgreSQL. This boundary keeps
database credentials and AI keys on the server and gives the application a central place for
authorization.

The main implementation areas are organized by domain. Company routes handle organization
structure and member management. Task routes and employee task pages cover assignment and
submission. Schedule routes cover meetings and attendance. Assessment actions own worksheet
creation, assignment, submission, grading, and resubmission. Forum routes own thread and reply
workflows. Company knowledge and assistant routes own ingestion and query operations. Analytics

                                                                      Ascent | Page 29
actions calculate performance views and request AI summaries. Shared libraries provide
authentication, database access, validation, audit events, email, analytics calculations, and Gemini

This organization follows feature boundaries rather than a separate backend service. The same
Next.js server executes page logic and server actions. The project statement proposed a FastAPI API
service; the repository instead uses the Next.js server runtime. This choice simplifies the prototype
deployment but means there is no independently deployed Python service or WebSocket API in the

4.20 Company Knowledge Ingestion and Retrieval Implementation
Knowledge entry begins with a company administrator or manager submitting a title and text. The
server limits the title to 160 characters, requires at least three characters in the title and 30 characters
of content, and rejects content above 50,000 characters. The text is divided into chunks with a target
maximum length of 1,200 characters. Paragraph boundaries are preferred; long paragraphs are split at
a nearby space when possible. Chunks are embedded in groups of up to four parallel API requests
and then written to the database in a transaction.

The stored record contains company identifier, creator membership, title, text chunk, embedding
JSON, and timestamps. The title is included in the text sent to the embedding endpoint, which
associates the passage with a short document label. A failure during embedding prevents the content
from being reported as successfully indexed. Deletion removes rows matching both the active
company and title. Because chunks share the title and there is no source-file model, document-level
version history is not currently represented.

On a user question, the action checks that the user has active company context and validates a
question length from 3 to 1,000 characters. It loads knowledge rows only for that company, embeds
the question, computes cosine similarity for each candidate, sorts candidates descending, and selects
at most four passages. The generation prompt includes the company name, a constraint to answer
only from supplied material, a direction to abstain when the answer is missing, and a reminder not to
disclose practice assessment answers. The result returns generated text and the unique titles of the
selected passages.

                                                                      Ascent | Page 33
The implementation is a small-corpus semantic retrieval path rather than a scalable vector database
design. All company embeddings are loaded for ranking in application memory. Query cost therefore
grows with the number of stored chunks. A future data layer could use pgvector or a managed vector
index, add retrieval thresholds and hybrid lexical search, preserve source page and section metadata,
and support safe re-indexing when documents change. Those improvements need a measured corpus
and evaluation set rather than assumptions about scale.

Code Snippet 4.1 shows the chunk-boundary policy used by the shared AI helper. Paragraphs are kept
together while they fit. A paragraph that is longer than the limit is split near a word boundary when
possible. The function is intentionally deterministic, which makes it suitable for unit testing and for
repeatable ingestion of the same text.

    export function splitIntoChunks(text: string, maxLength = 1200): string[] {
      const normalized = text.replace(/\r\n/g, "\n").trim();
      const chunks: string[] = [];
      let current = "";
      for (const paragraph of normalized.split(/\n{2,}/)) {
          let remaining = paragraph.trim();
          while (remaining.length > maxLength) {
             const cut = remaining.lastIndexOf(" ", maxLength);
             const index = cut > maxLength * 0.6 ? cut : maxLength;
             const part = remaining.slice(0, index).trim();
             if (current) chunks.push(current);
             chunks.push(part);
             current = "";
             remaining = remaining.slice(index).trim();
          }
          if (!remaining) continue;
          if ((current ? current.length + 2 : 0) + remaining.length > maxLength) {
             if (current) chunks.push(current);
             current = remaining;
          } else {
             current = current ? current + "\n\n" + remaining : remaining;
          }
      }
      if (current) chunks.push(current);
      return chunks;

   }

Code Snippet 4.2 is the vector comparison used to rank stored passages. It rejects vectors with
different dimensions and treats empty or zero-magnitude input as a zero score. This behavior
prevents an invalid vector from producing a misleading numeric result. The function does not apply a
relevance threshold: it always orders the available candidates and the caller takes the highest-ranked
passages.

                                                                      Ascent | Page 34
    export function cosineSimilarity(a: number[], b: number[]): number {
      if (!a.length || a.length !== b.length) return 0;
      let dot = 0;
      let normA = 0;
      let normB = 0;
      for (let i = 0; i < a.length; i += 1) {
          dot += a[i] * b[i];
          normA += a[i] * a[i];
          normB += b[i] * b[i];
      }
      return normA && normB ? dot / (Math.sqrt(normA) * Math.sqrt(normB)) : 0;

   }

      .sort((a, b) => b.score - a.score)
      .slice(0, 4);

The retrieved passages become the evidence context for generation. The application extracts distinct
titles from the selected passages and returns those titles alongside the answer. A title is a source
pointer, not a line-level citation: the current record does not retain page number, paragraph offset, or
original-file location. The ingestion and retrieval design is therefore appropriate for a demonstration
of grounded company Q&A, while a production knowledge service would require stronger
provenance and more extensive evaluation.

4.21 AI Insight Actions and Output Controls

The AI integration is isolated in a shared Gemini helper. The helper reads its API key from server
environment configuration, posts JSON to the model endpoint, uses a configurable embedding model
with a default of gemini-embedding-001, and uses a configurable text model with a default of
gemini-2.5-flash. Generation requests use low temperature and a maximum output-token limit.
Empty embeddings and empty text responses produce errors. JSON generation requests ask for JSON
only, then attempt a direct parse and a limited object extraction fallback.

                                                                      Ascent | Page 35
The grading action and doubt-insight action use the shared helper but have different inputs and
outputs. Grading is associated with an individual assessment and validated against mark bounds.
Doubt analysis selects up to 100 recent open or answered threads for the company. If the caller is a
manager, the selection is further limited to direct reports. The model is asked to produce up to six
themes with a topic, estimated count, short summary, and practical suggestion. The server truncates
strings and bounds counts before returning the result.

These controls bound output size and scope; they do not verify factual correctness. A generated
theme may be too broad, and a count is an estimate rather than a validated count of distinct
employees. The underlying threads should be reviewed before follow-up. The system does not
automatically change a task, assessment grade, employee status, or policy based on generated
content.

4.22 Analytics and Progress Calculation

Progress analytics use recorded task assignments, delivery state, and released assessments. When
task and assessment data are both present, the composite formula weights assessment performance at
45 percent, task completion at 35 percent, and on-time delivery at 20 percent. For task-only records,
task completion receives 65 percent and on-time delivery 35 percent. Insufficient data remains
unranked instead of being interpreted as a failing score.

4.23 Notifications, Audit, and Error Handling

The project integrates invitation and workflow notifications through configured email providers.
Email is an external dependency and may not be available in every local setup. Audit helpers record
selected high-impact operations with actor, company, action, entity type, entity identifier, and
structured metadata. Audit records support traceability but do not by themselves provide a complete
security monitoring system.

                          Ascent | Page 36
Validation failures return user-facing errors before database mutation. Provider failures are caught in
AI actions and returned as an error result. Database writes that create related records use transactions
where implemented. Route revalidation updates pages after successful mutation. Production
hardening should standardize error codes, avoid returning provider response detail to end users, apply
retry and rate-limit policies, and ensure logs never contain secret keys or unnecessary sensitive text.
4.24 Development and Execution Structure
The repository uses the Next.js application as the main runtime. Prisma provides the generated client
and schema management for PostgreSQL. Environment files supply database, identity, email, AI
model, and test-portal settings. Seed scripts create general development data and the targeted demo
knowledge corpus. The local execution path therefore requires the application dependencies, a
reachable configured database, a synchronized schema, and the environment values needed for the
feature being demonstrated.
The knowledge seeder is separate from the general application seed and is designed for the three
demonstration documents. After database schema synchronization, it creates or refreshes searchable
chunks for the Ascent Demo Workspace. Keeping demo knowledge seeding separate avoids making
the normal user seed responsible for external AI calls. A repeatable seed path is useful for evaluation
because it allows the assistant corpus to be restored without manually re-entering each item.

5.12 Threats to Validity

The evaluation uses a single repository snapshot and a small synthetic knowledge corpus. Automated
tests cover selected logic, not every UI route or external service response. Gemini output can vary, so
an answer observed once cannot establish repeatable quality. Demo data is fictional and does not
represent a representative employee population. Performance analytics have not been validated
against manager ratings or onboarding outcomes. These factors limit generalization and prevent
claims about real-world effectiveness.

The requirements comparison also involves interpretation. A general task may partially support a
role-specific activity but does not satisfy an interactive simulator requirement. A named discussion
can support questions but does not satisfy anonymity. To reduce ambiguity, this report names the
implemented mechanism and the missing property separately rather than assigning full completion
based on a similar label.

Chapter 6: Conclusion, Summary and Future Scope

6.1 Project Summary

Ascent is a multi-tenant employee onboarding and performance platform built with Next.js, React,
TypeScript, Neon PostgreSQL, Prisma, Clerk, and Gemini. It brings company administration,
onboarding work, meetings, assessments, discussion, analytics, and AI assistance into a role-aware
workspace. Server-side company context and authorization are central to its design.

6.2 Conclusion

The project delivers a substantial prototype for organizing employee onboarding and manager
follow-up. It provides structured work and review, schedule management, assessment workflows,
discussion tools, analytics, and company-specific AI answers. Unit-level verification and the seeded
company demonstration show the main data and utility paths are functioning. The project statement’s
goals around document uploads, anonymous questions, and dedicated work simulation are not yet
fully met and should be completed before the system is described as a full implementation of that
statement.

6.3 Limitations

The current knowledge interface requires text to be pasted manually and does not preserve original
file attachments or document versions. The assistant uses JSON-stored embeddings with in-process
cosine similarity and has not been evaluated against a formal benchmark. Forum authors are visible.
The work-simulation experience is composed from tasks and assessments rather than a scenario
engine. Production security, performance, availability, and deployment claims require independent
testing.

6.4 Future Scope

  1. Add secure PDF, DOCX, and other document upload with extraction, validation, versioning, and
     deletion controls.

  2. Add a proper semantic-search index, including PostgreSQL pgvector if chosen, with company-
     scoped retrieval and migration tooling.

  3. Add configurable anonymous questions with manager workflows that protect author identity
     while maintaining abuse controls.

  4. Build a scenario-based work-simulation module with branching situations, role-specific tasks,
     evidence submissions, and manager rubrics.

  5. Generate onboarding roadmaps from role, department, and program duration, while allowing
     managers to review and edit the result.

                                                                      Ascent | Page 45
  6. Add a test corpus for retrieval quality, answer grounding, unsupported questions, prompt
     injection, and cross-company isolation.

  7. Add browser-based end-to-end tests, deployment automation, monitoring, backups, and
     operational runbooks.

  8. Evaluate privacy, retention, AI provider settings, latency, and per-company usage costs before
     production rollout.

6.5 Contribution to Organizational Development

Ascent supports more consistent onboarding by connecting policies, tasks, meetings, assessments,
employee questions, and manager oversight. The AI functions can make company guidance easier to
search and summarize repeated questions. These contributions are demonstrative; measurable
improvements in completion time, employee confidence, or retention would require a real
deployment and a user study.

6.6 Recommended Development Roadmap
The next phase should begin with requirements closure rather than additional AI features. First,
implement a role-and-duration roadmap model that managers can edit before publishing. Second,
build a controlled historical-work simulation with sanitized artifacts and a scoring rubric. Third,
design anonymous question handling with clear disclosure, moderation controls, and privacy review.
These changes address central requirements in the original statement.

The next phase of AI work should improve the ingestion and evaluation pipeline. Add file upload
with type and size validation, safe text extraction, source metadata, version replacement, and
deletion. Add a retrieval threshold and a vector index after measuring the expected corpus size. Build
a gold question set with answerable, ambiguous, conflicting, and unanswerable examples. Evaluate
retrieved evidence and generated response separately, and include prompt-injection and tenant-
isolation cases.

Before production use, run browser end-to-end tests for each role; perform a security review of every
server action; define retention, consent, and data-processing rules; exercise backups and restoration;
and measure latency, provider cost, and rate limits. A pilot should collect user feedback and task
completion evidence, with a comparison against the organization’s current onboarding process. These
steps would turn a demonstrator into an evidence-based product evaluation.

6.7 Contribution to Sustainable Development Goals
The project has a direct connection to organizational learning and workplace development rather than
to a measured sustainability outcome. Its platform can help make onboarding information and
learning activities more accessible, which is relevant to quality education and skills development as a
broad objective. However, the application does not measure energy use, emissions, or environmental
impact and should not claim direct contribution to climate targets.

                                                                      Ascent | Page 46
Its potential societal value lies in supporting clearer role expectations and access to information for
new employees. Whether this improves well-being, inclusion, or retention must be established
through real deployment and ethical evaluation. A future pilot should include accessibility, language,
and privacy considerations so the platform does not create barriers for employees who need different
formats or communication methods.
